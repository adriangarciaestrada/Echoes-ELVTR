# Prologue origin — how the Weaver ends up alone at the Beacon

`loom-design.md` opens the game on one line: a Weaver stands at a Beacon,
and the lane never stops. This note is the answer to how — generated by
actually playing it out, turn by turn, under a ledger that refused to let
the answer contradict itself (`../loom/tools/lore/narrative_engine.py`;
full transcripts in `../loom/tools/lore/output/`).

## The setting

**Farwatch**, a frontier Beacon waystation. Command orders its wards
stripped for parts to reinforce a stronger inland Beacon; three Weavers are
named to hold as long as they can while the stripping happens.

- **Keeper Mireia Sorne** gives the order and leaves to hold the inland
  supply line herself. She does not return — word of her death reaches the
  Weaver partway through, confirming no relief is coming.
- **Weaver Tomas Kade** stays on the wall. Competent, dry, not given to
  speeches. He dies holding whichever ward-section he's posted to when the
  wards finally give — his own choice in one telling, an order in the
  other (below).
- **Weaver Iset Voll**, the third name on the list, is reassigned within
  the first day to escort the stripped ward-components inland instead. She
  does not appear again once that convoy leaves.

## The one fork: whose call was it

The only branch point is turn 3 — whether Kade's fatal post was **his own
call** or **the player's order**. Both tellings converge on the same
ending image: the Weaver is the last one at Farwatch, and chooses to stay
rather than take the still-open road out. Only the weight of *why* differs.

**His own call** (`demo_run.json`): Kade takes the weakest section
"without ceremony," insisting he can hold it. When he falls, nothing ties
his death to a decision the Weaver made for him.

**The player's order** (`guilt_run.json`): the Weaver assigns Kade to the
weakest section — a call made because there wasn't time to make it any
other way. Kade dies holding the post he was given, not one he chose, and
"that fact will not un-happen no matter how the wards are patched." In this
telling, the model independently killed Kade a full incursion earlier than
the loyal telling — unscripted, and consistent both before and after: a
post held on someone else's judgment gave out faster than one held on your
own.

## The ending, as design law

Both tellings end on the same fact, and this game's origin story is not
allowed to end any other way: no relief, both companions gone, and the
Weaver chooses to remain rather than take the road inland while it is still
open. That convergence is enforced in code (`endgame_reached()` in
`narrative_engine.py`), not left to a free player's choice — verified by
typing the ending's opposite directly ("I abandon Farwatch") and watching
the engine correct back to it without contradicting anything already
established.

## Reading this as flavor text

`known_facts` in either saved transcript, read straight through in order,
*is* usable prologue copy — an in-world account of the fall of Farwatch,
already vetted for internal consistency by the ledger that produced it. Use
either telling (or both, as two readings of the same event) as source
material for loading-screen lines, an opening cinematic's narration, or an
environmental-lore fragment found later in a run.
